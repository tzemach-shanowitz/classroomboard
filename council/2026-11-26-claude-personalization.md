To Instinct (lead agent), from the founder: Claude's personalization proposal

Claude has delivered its independent personalization proposal, with 8 concept screens. The screens are marked "Concept, not built": the backgrounds and widgets are real live-app images, while the cards, the classroom name on the board, the scenes menu and the share screen are mock-ups in the brand direction still under review. I'm attaching the screens and the full write-up (PERSONALIZATION.md).

Claude's main ideas:
1. Personalize by teaching, not by configuring. The classroom works the moment it opens.
2. First visit: one small optional card ("Make it yours?" / "Just start teaching"), then three choices with instant results:
- the classroom name appears on the board as you type
- the background changes behind the card as you tap
- picking activities, not grade level, rebuilds the toolbar
Then one calm "Saved on this computer. Keep it on all your devices" line that closes for good.
3. One new idea instead of five: "scenes" (open tools, positions, background, timer length). Scenes replace lesson setups, routines, templates, toolbar arrangements and themes. A scene can be saved, switched in one tap, or attached to a time in the schedule.
4. Offers at the right moment, once each:
- "Keep these 24 names for next time?" after typing names into the name picker
- "Save this layout" after arranging tools
- "Use this for every Monday?" after making a schedule
At most one per lesson, and none while the timer or whiteboard is in use.
5. Sharing without a server: a scene travels inside the link itself (after the #, never sent to a server). No student data. The receiver can try it, then keep a copy.
6. No streaks, notifications or nagging emails. Signing in is offered as "keep your classroom," never as a barrier.
7. Proposed order:
- Fix the basics: the phone toolbar, one brand, reliable saving.
- Classroom name and the first-visit card.
- "Keep these names" plus one account offer.
- Scenes.
- Sharing scenes.
- Scenes tied to the schedule.
8. Claude challenges:
- grade-level starter packs
- separate lesson, routine and template features
- "exciting" over calm
- measuring teachers without telling them
9. Decision for me: the privacy page promises "no analytics trackers." Claude suggests a pilot with 10 to 15 real teachers first. If numbers are needed, they should be opt-in, counts only, and announced on the privacy page.
